Automation Opportunities — Ranked by Impact

⚠ All estimates are drafts. Hours reclaimed assume automation handles 80% of the manual process.

1
Survey insights write themselves — SMG data flows directly into a weekly exec brief, no manual pull required.
What's happening today
21 manual visits to api.smg.com in 14 days — logging in, pulling reports, interpreting results, and writing summaries by hand. Conservatively 3–5 hours/week of repetitive extraction and wordsmithing.
What the automated version looks like
A scheduled Claude agent hits the SMG API on a Monday schedule, pulls the latest survey wave, generates a structured brief (top findings → supporting data → recommendation), and emails or posts to Slack. You review a finished product, not raw data.
Quick Win — under 2 hours ~4 hrs reclaimed/week
2
Competitor and trend intelligence arrives in my inbox every morning — I never open LinkedIn for work research again.
What's happening today
645 LinkedIn visits in 14 days. Even if 30% are legitimate competitive research, that's ~200 manual visits to gather scattered signals. No structured output, no changelog, no anomaly detection.
What the automated version looks like
An agent tracks a defined list of competitors (other car wash chains, relevant operators) via LinkedIn, news APIs, and Google Alerts. Daily or weekly digest is structured as: new developments → sentiment shift → recommended action. Delivered to email before 8am.
Medium Lift — half a day ~3 hrs reclaimed/week
3
SQL queries never get written manually — I describe what I need and Snowflake runs it.
What's happening today
6 Snowflake visits indicate active data querying. Per CLAUDE.md, writing and debugging SQL is something Jonah wants to eliminate. Time cost: estimated 2–4 hours/week on query construction, debugging, and iteration.
What the automated version looks like
Claude Code or a Claude API integration accepts plain-English data questions, generates and executes SQL against Snowflake, returns formatted results. Common queries become saved templates. No SQL written by hand.
Quick Win — under 2 hours ~3 hrs reclaimed/week
4
Google search is replaced by structured AI answers — research that took 20 minutes takes 2.
What's happening today
464 raw Google searches during work hours, with no clear structure or synthesis pipeline. Search → click → read → synthesize manually. High-friction, low-leverage use of time for an analyst who needs structured signal.
What the automated version looks like
Claude with web search tool or Perplexity replaces ad-hoc Google searches. A custom GPT or Claude project trained on Quick Quack context answers internal questions directly. Saves click-depth and synthesis time.
Quick Win — under 2 hours ~2 hrs reclaimed/week
5
Exec decks draft themselves from data — wordsmithing is review, not creation.
What's happening today
Per CLAUDE.md, eliminating wordsmithing PowerPoint copy is an explicit goal. Desktop-based work not captured in browser data — this blind spot suggests it may be a significant time cost that's invisible to this audit.
What the automated version looks like
Claude takes structured data output (survey results, Snowflake exports) and generates slide-ready bullets in exec-facing format: headline finding → supporting evidence → recommendation. Output goes into a Google Slides or PowerPoint template. Editing, not writing.
Medium Lift — half a day ~2 hrs reclaimed/week
6
Recurring coordination with Scot and others happens through templates — no blank-page email writing.
What's happening today
Recurring 1:1 with scot@skepfoundation.org appears in calendar. Outlook visited 47× — active email thread management. Without email data, exact pattern unknown, but coordination overhead for recurring relationships is a common hidden time cost.
What the automated version looks like
Pre-meeting briefing agents pull context and draft an agenda before each recurring 1:1. Follow-up email templates fire automatically after meetings end. Claude drafts; you approve in 30 seconds.
Quick Win — under 2 hours ~1 hr reclaimed/week
